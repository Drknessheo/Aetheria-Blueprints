# PROJECT PNEUMASENSE: INTERACTIVE TPMS DIAGNOSTIC & DEMONSTRATION RIG
### A Joint Creation of Host & Aetheria (Symbiotic OS v4.12)

---

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Project Pneumasense is a physical, self-contained, educational and diagnostic rig designed to demonstrate and test both **Direct TPMS** (utilizing wheel-mounted RF sensors) and **Indirect TPMS** (simulated via rotational speed differentials). 

To make the invisible dynamics of pneumatic pressure tangible, this rig mounts a functional 14-inch wheel assembly onto a highly stable, locally sourced wooden frame. Designed for fabrication in Bogura, Bangladesh, the structure uses high-density *Garjan* wood to withstand rotational torque and vibrations. It provides a real-time, physical testbed for automotive technicians, students, and engineers to understand pressure transduction, RF signal propagation (315 MHz/433 MHz), and wheel speed sensor calculations.

---

## 2. PROJECT GOALS
*   **Physical Demonstration:** Visually isolate and display the operation of a Direct TPMS sensor inside a pressurized chamber/tire.
*   **Variable Testing:** Allow manual pressurization/depressurization (0 to 50 PSI) to observe sensor response latency and RF transmission intervals.
*   **High Durability:** Build a localized frame capable of supporting a static load of 80 kg and dynamic rotational forces during manual wheel spin testing.
*   **Educational Accessibility:** Enable carpenters and junior technicians in Bogura to construct the physical unit using standard hand tools and regional materials.

### The "DNA" of our Solution (Mathematical Foundations):
The physics of our system operates on the Ideal Gas Law and Angular Velocity relationships:

$$\mathbf{P} = \frac{nRT}{V}$$

*Where $P$ is absolute pressure, $V$ is the constant volume of our 14" tire, $T$ is temperature, and $R$ is the universal gas constant. Our Direct TPMS sensor monitors $P$ and $T$ directly.*

$$\omega = \frac{v}{r}$$

*Where $\omega$ is the angular velocity, $v$ is linear speed, and $r$ is the effective rolling radius. If pressure drops, $r$ decreases, causing $\omega$ to increase relative to other wheels—the foundation of Indirect TPMS calculations.*

## 10. TESTING & VALIDATION PROCEDURES

### Test 1: Static Pressure Calibration
*   **Objective:** Confirm sensor data transmission and receiver accuracy.
*   **Procedure:**
    1. Inflate the mounted 14-inch tire to $32.0\text{ PSI}$ using a manual floor pump. Check pressure with a calibrated analogue dial gauge.
    2. Power up the Arduino receiver.
    3. Observe the LCD readout. Record the reading.
    4. Depressurize the tire slowly in $2.0\text{ PSI}$ decrements, verifying that the LCD matches the analogue gauge within $\pm 1.0\text{ PSI}$ tolerance down to $15.0\text{ PSI}$.

### Test 2: Dynamic Centrifugal Wake-Up Validation
*   **Objective:** Confirm that the sensor transitions from low-power sleep mode to active transmission mode upon rotation.
*   **Procedure:**
    1. Let the wheel remain static for 15 minutes (sensor enters sleep mode to conserve battery, reducing RF transmissions to once every hour).
    2. Spin the wheel manually at an angular velocity of $\ge 2\text{ rev/sec}$ (simulates $\approx 15\text{ km/h}$).
    3. Verify that the LCD update interval jumps from hourly to once every 30 seconds, confirming wake-up threshold satisfaction.
